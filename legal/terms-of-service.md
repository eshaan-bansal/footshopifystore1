# Terms of Service — ready to paste

Paste into **Shopify admin → Settings → Policies → Terms of service**. Replace
every `[BRACKETED]` value.

Not legal advice, and not reviewed by an attorney. Have one look before you take
real orders.

**The clauses doing the real work for this store**, so you know what not to delete:

- **§3 Orders** — an order is *your offer*, accepted only when we dispatch. This
  is what lets you decline an order (pricing error, supplier out of stock,
  suspected fraud) without breaching a contract.
- **§9 No medical claims** — the single most important clause here. It states
  plainly that these are comfort products, not medical devices, and that nothing
  on the site is medical advice. It backs up everything in COMPLIANCE.md.
- **§11 Reviews** — reserves the right to remove fake or incentivised reviews.
  Under the FTC's Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials you are responsible
  for review content you publish, so you need the right to take it down.
- **§13 Limitation of liability** — caps exposure at the amount paid.

## Optional clause — arbitration and class action waiver

Many US stores add this. It is genuinely protective, but the trade-off is real, so
decide deliberately rather than copying it in by default.

> **Arbitration.** Any dispute arising from these Terms or your purchase will be
> resolved by binding individual arbitration administered by [AAA/JAMS] under its
> consumer rules, rather than in court. **You and we waive any right to a jury
> trial and to participate in a class action.** You may opt out of this clause by
> emailing [SUPPORT@YOURDOMAIN.COM] within 30 days of your first order.

**For:** avoids class actions, which are the expensive scenario for a consumer
product business.

**Against:** you normally pay most arbitration fees, so even meritless individual
claims cost you money. Coordinated "mass arbitration" — hundreds of individual
filings at once — has become a deliberate tactic and can cost more than the class
action it was meant to avoid. Enforceability also depends on the opt-out and on
clear presentation at checkout.

If you include it, keep the 30-day opt-out. It materially improves the odds the
clause survives a challenge.
